Cyber Resilience Checklist

Cybersecurity · Checklist

What to ask about cyber risk

An attack moves through three stages. Read left to right: each card is a question to put to the teams and vendors who run your systems, with what a good answer sounds like.

Stage 1 · Keep them out

Where could someone get in?

1

What is exposed to the internet?

Look forA written inventory of VPNs, remote-access tools, admin panels, app servers and test environments, produced within a week.

2

Are VPNs and remote access patched, with MFA?

Look forA named owner, patches measured in days, and MFA on every admin and remote login, ideally hardware security keys.

3

Does the server enforce authorization?

Look forEvery request is checked against the specific record, so one customer can't read another's data (BOLA/IDOR), and someone outside the team has tested it.

Stage 2 · Limit the damage

If they do, how far can they go?

4

Why is this data still retained?

Look forA retention policy that is enforced, with dormant and cancelled accounts purged or anonymized.

5

Who has privileged access?

Look forThe company owns the cloud accounts and keys. Vendor logins are named, least-privilege and expiring, with logs the vendor can't erase. Contracts set patch SLAs, audit rights and breach notice.

6

Is access revoked when people leave?

Look forSame-day deprovisioning for staff and vendors through SSO, with a quarterly access review.

Stage 3 · Recover and tell

Could the company recover and speak up?

7

Could the company restore tonight?

Look forImmutable backups in a separate account and region (3-2-1 as the baseline), and a restore that has been practiced.

8

Who declares an incident?

Look forA named incident owner, a response plan that has been drilled, and legal knows the regulator's reporting deadlines (APPI in Japan).

9

What will customers be told?

Look forA public list of what the company never asks for, official channels only, and a way to report phishing and tampered QR codes.

Across all three stages · People

Train for fewer mistakes. Build so each one costs less.

Email links and attachments

Train
Make reporting a suspicious message easy and blame-free. Run realistic phishing simulations.
Build in
Filter mail and sandbox links and attachments. Use phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2 or passkeys) and least-privilege accounts, so one click can't reach everything.

USB and other external devices

Train
Never plug in a device of unknown origin, including one that was found or handed over.
Build in
Use endpoint device control: block USB mass storage by default, allow approved encrypted drives, disable auto-run, and scan with endpoint protection.

Unapproved apps and AI tools

Train
Explain which data must never leave, and make the approved route quick so nobody goes around it.
Build in
Discover unsanctioned SaaS and AI tools, put approved ones behind SSO, and apply data loss prevention (DLP) to uploads.

Training reduces how often mistakes happen. Built-in protection limits what one mistake can do. Ask for both.

Before launch

Automated checks on every release, a person's review with evidence for the rest, and a written executive sign-off for any exception. Put the cost in the project estimate from day one.

A starting point for conversations with technical teams and vendors. The right questions will vary with data sensitivity and how the business runs.
